## Encoding decisions (not uncertainties: the glyph is clear, but LaTeX offers several ways to write it)

1. **Inflation target: hat over bar over π** — (62), (64), (94), and inline at p.26. The paper prints a **wide** hat (the `\widehat` glyph) over a **full-width** bar (`\overline`-like). This is visibly different from the narrow `\hat{\bar{\pi}}` used in Appendix D (p.120, the EXAMPLE equation 229). Stacking candidates under the paper crop: `\widehat{\overline{\pi}}` reproduces the printed glyph exactly; `\hat{\bar{\pi}}` has a smaller hat and bar. The symbol and its meaning are the same in both cases (hat = log deviation, bar = target), and CONVENTIONS §4.5 and the EXAMPLE prescribe `\hat{\bar{\pi}}` for "a hat printed over a bar over π", so I encoded **`\hat{\bar{\pi}}`** throughout (including `\hat{\bar{\pi}}_{t}^{*}` in (94)). This keeps one symbol name across chunks. The renderer will show a narrower hat than the paper; that difference is typographic only.
   - Comparison images: (working image, not included) c64.png, c94bar.png; reference p.120: c120pibar.png.
2. **Shock superscript in (64)**: printed $\eta_{t}^{\overline{\Pi}}$ with a full-width bar over Π. Encoded `\eta_{t}^{\bar{\Pi}}`, the same symbol (`\bar` comes out narrower in render). Comparison: c64.png.
3. **Wide hats on single letters in the definition of the real rate** (p.26): $\widehat{ri}_{t} \equiv \hat{r}_{t} - E_{t}\hat{\pi}_{C,t+1}$ — the hats over r and π are printed wide (`\widehat`-style), unlike the narrow hats in (62) and (65). Per CONVENTIONS §4.5 (`\hat` for a single letter), encoded `\hat{r}_{t}` and `\hat{\pi}_{C,t+1}`, the same variables as in (62)/(65). Comparison: c63ri.png.
4. **Multi-letter wide hats**: `\widehat{rr}` (forward real rate), `\widehat{ri}` (real rate), `\widehat{wt}` (world trade): each is printed with one wide hat spanning both letters.
5. **Upright Y in subscripts**: in several places the subscript of $P_{Y,t}$ is printed in an upright (roman) Y, e.g. the inline share definitions on p.25 and p.27, the profit-share display on p.32, and the p.39/p.40 definitions with $P_{Y,t}^{*}$, while in other places it is italic. I treated it as the same label Y everywhere (`P_{Y,t}`). This is a font inconsistency in the paper, not a different symbol.
6. **Stars in coefficient subscripts** (89), (92), (94): the asterisk is raised (superscript) after y, π, r inside the subscripts, so I wrote `c_{y^{*},+}`, `c_{\pi^{*},+}`, `c_{r^{*},-}`, etc. The same holds for `\varepsilon_{t}^{Y^{*}}`, `^{WT^{*}}`, `^{\Pi^{*}}`, `^{R^{*}}`, and `f^{*}` in (76.i1)–(79). Checked against renders: c89c/c92c/c94c in combo_c.png, combo_stars.png, c77f.png.
7. **Minus signs in coefficient subscripts**: `c_{wt,-}`, `c_{oil,-}` and `c_{r^{*},-}` are printed with a genuine minus sign (ASCII `-` used, per §4.4). `c_{wt,y\_lag}` has a literal underscore, printed as a text underscore; encoded `y\_lag`. Comparison: cwt.png.
8. **(94) brackets**: the square bracket opens on line 1 and closes on line 3. I used plain `[` `]` (no `\left`/`\right`) so the bracket can span the `aligned` line breaks. The round parentheses in line 2 are large in print and are written with `\left(`…`\right)`.
9. **(88)**: line 1 ends with "=" and line 2 begins with "=", as printed; both are kept.

## Suspected typos / inconsistencies in the paper (transcribed as printed)

1. **(89)**: a stray subscript **t** is printed after the parenthesis, $\left(1-c_{y^{*},+}\right)_{t}\hat{y}_{t-1}^{*}$. It is almost certainly a typo: the coefficient should be $(1-c_{y^{*},+})$ with no time index. Transcribed as printed. Zoom: (working image, not included)
2. **(65) vs Appendix D (265)**: the main text defines $\hat{\pi}_{t}^{CB}=E_{t}[\hat{\pi}_{C,t-2}+\hat{\pi}_{C,t-1}+\hat{\pi}_{C,t}+\hat{\pi}_{C,t+1}]$ as a plain **sum** of four quarters. Appendix D (265), p.119 (PDF 125), prints **0.25×** the same four terms (an average). One of the two is inconsistent; the replication should check which was used in estimation. Transcribed as printed.
3. **(63) vs Appendix D (262)**: (63) averages $\widehat{ri}_{t+21}$ … $\widehat{ri}_{t+40}$ (20 terms, i.e. 5–10 years ahead). Appendix D (262), p.118 (PDF 124), prints $\frac{1}{20}E_{t}(\widehat{ri}_{t+20}+\ldots+\widehat{ri}_{t+39})$, an index shift of one quarter. Also, (63.i1) defines $\widehat{ri}_{t}\equiv\hat{r}_{t}-E_{t}\hat{\pi}_{C,t+1}$, while Appendix D (261) omits the $E_{t}$. Transcribed as printed in Section 3.
4. **Output-gap definition (62.i1)**: $\hat{y}_{t}^{GAP}\equiv\log\frac{Y_{t}}{Z_{t}\varepsilon_{t}}-\log y$ uses an **upper-case $Z_{t}$**, but productivity is lower-case $z_{t}$ everywhere else (e.g. $g_{t}\equiv G_{t}/z_{t}$, (83), (88)). Probably a typo for $z_{t}$. Transcribed as printed (checked against a rendered `Z` vs `z`: cZ.png). Note also that Appendix D (264) writes $y_{t}^{GAP}=\hat{y}_{t}-\hat{\epsilon}_{t}$ with a lunate epsilon.
5. **Import shock glyph**: (85) and (86) print the import-productivity shock as lunate **$\epsilon_{t}^{IM}$**, while its defining equation (38) on p.25 (chunk T03) uses **$\varepsilon_{t}^{IM}$**. It is the same shock. Transcribed as printed (`\epsilon`). Comparison: c85eps.png.
6. **(58)**: $\tau^{W_{f}}$ is printed without a time subscript, unlike the other tax rates, both in (58) and in the list of constant rates on p.25. It is constant, so this is harmless. Transcribed as printed.
7. **(58) vs (81)**: the utilization-cost argument is aggregate $u_{t}$ in (58) but household $u_{h,t}$ in (81), (85) and (86). Transcribed as printed.
8. **(88) line 2**: the domestic marginal-cost term uses $H_{f,t}$ (no superscript s) next to $H_{f,t}^{s}$ in the revenue term. Transcribed as printed.
9. **(68)**: a trailing period is printed below the baseline after the fraction, and it was dropped (§4.13). Similarly, the odd spacing before the trailing "." or "," in (76), (80), (82), (85), (86) was dropped.
